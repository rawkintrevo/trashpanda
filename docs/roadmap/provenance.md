# Proposal: provenance that travels with the model

**Status: proposed; not an implemented artifact contract.**

[Roadmap](README.md) · [Current metadata](../implementation/provenance.md)

## Outcome

An organization receiving a model can inspect which data and parent artifacts
were used, when training took place, what configuration and software produced it,
and which approval or attestation claims accompany it—without needing the
original training machine.

“Part of the model” means a versioned provenance document bundled with and
cryptographically bound to the model files. It does not mean encoding evidence
in neural weights or relying on a generated answer to describe training history.
Native Hugging Face loading should remain possible without executing custom code.

## Proposed implementation

1. **Input records.** Identify each dataset by source, immutable revision where
   available, content hashes, preparation rules, split membership, and tokenizer
   revision. Reference the organization's usage terms and approval evidence,
   including restrictions, reviewer, date, and declared unknowns. Keep proprietary
   paths/content out of public records by default.
2. **Run records generated by the trainer.** Record run ID, UTC start/end times,
   code revision and dirty-state indication, dependency/environment identity,
   resolved configuration, actual completed targets, repeated passes, checkpoint
   ancestry, and evaluation settings. Bind these to verified prepared-data identity;
   do not populate actual counts from the requested budget.
3. **Artifact binding.** Ship a schema-versioned record with checkpoint and HF
   export, containing model/tokenizer/config hashes and references to input/run
   records. Hash payload files first; keep the record's own digest/signature outside
   its hashed payload to avoid circular hashes. Preserve or extend ancestry on
   resume, export, and future continuation/fine-tuning.
4. **Local verification.** Validate schemas and hashes offline; distinguish
   missing evidence, corrupt files, self-asserted claims, and verified signatures.
   Use explicit policy decisions for unknown or disallowed inputs. Verification
   must not silently label a dataset or model “safe.”
5. **Interoperability.** Evaluate mapping to Sourcelume's dataset and training
   records. Keep registry publication optional and separately authorized. A local
   portable record should not require a public registry or disclosure of private data.

The format, CLI, signing mechanism, and enforcement defaults are design proposals,
not existing APIs. Do not add unverifiable fields merely to make a record look complete.

## Security and governance decisions still needed

- Who is authorized to approve inputs, and who trusts their attestations?
- Which fields are public, organization-private, or participant-private?
- How are approval withdrawal and new dataset concerns communicated for old artifacts?
- How are inherited unknowns from pretrained weights represented?
- What can be verified offline versus requiring a registry or approval service?
- Which Sourcelume schema/version and optional signing/identity system should be used?

## Acceptance evidence

- Train on two identified datasets and derive counts/lineage from the actual run.
- Copy only the export bundle to a fresh, offline machine; inspect provenance and
  verify payload identity without the original cache, registry, or training directory.
- Tamper with weights, tokenizer, or a referenced input identity: verification fails.
- Missing approvals remain explicitly unknown or blocked under the selected policy;
  schema validity and hashes never stand in for legal review.
- Resume and re-export preserve ancestry and add truthful run/time information.

These requirements extend [First Model](first-model.md), whose current hashes and
checkpoint state are useful building blocks but not this complete provenance system.
